# Brume vs Heroku: research notes

Checked: 2026-11-01. Official sources only: heroku.com, devcenter.heroku.com, help.heroku.com, github.com/heroku. "not found" means no official source confirmed it.

**What it is:** Heroku is Salesforce's managed, closed-source PaaS. Apps are deployed with `git push` and built by buildpacks, then run in "dynos" (managed Linux containers) on AWS, next to managed Postgres, Key-Value Store and an add-on marketplace. Since February 2026 it has been in a "sustaining engineering" model.

## Where it is better than a self-hosted single-server alpha

1. **Managed Postgres with real recovery.** Continuous WAL archiving to S3, point-in-time rollback (4 days to 1 week), followers and HA on Premium and above, all run by Heroku. Brume 0.1 backs up to your S3 bucket, but you operate the database and the restore.
2. **Certified compliance.** SOC 1/2/3 and ISO 27001/27017/27018 across runtimes, and PCI DSS Level 1 and HIPAA with a BAA on Shield. A single-admin alpha has no audits.
3. **Horizontal scale and isolation.** Many dynos per process type, autoscaling on Performance and above, dynos up to 126 GB RAM, and network-isolated Private Spaces in 10 regions. One VPS is one machine and one failure domain.
4. **18 years of maturity and an ecosystem.** A well-known `git push heroku` workflow, 200+ add-ons, open-source buildpacks, pipelines, and Enterprise support contracts that Salesforce still honors. This holds even though Heroku is now in sustaining mode.
5. **No server to run.** OS patching, routing, TLS and dyno restarts are Heroku's job. With Brume you own the VPS, its updates and its uptime.

## Suggested verdict

Heroku is the mature managed PaaS with managed Postgres, autoscaling and certified compliance, now in sustaining mode with per-dyno pricing. Brume is the free, alpha, single-server option where apps, data and backups stay on your own VPS and S3 bucket at a fixed cost.
